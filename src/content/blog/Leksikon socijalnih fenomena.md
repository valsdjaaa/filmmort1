**The Social Lexicon**

**1. Core Currencies**

- **Social Capital:** The stock of trust, reciprocity, and value you hold within a network. Your "reputation credit." Operators seek to accumulate it.
    
- **Spiritual Capital:** A more potent, rarer currency. The perceived depth, authenticity, and moral authority of a person. The Apostle's primary currency. Hard to earn, devastating when weaponized.
    
- **Emotional Capital:** Investment in bonds, vulnerability, and shared feelings. The Mermaid's specialty. Often used to create obligation.
    
- **Vulnerability Capital:** The strategic sharing of weakness or need to trigger a protective or investment response from others. A key tool in the Apostle's and Mermaid's arsenal.
    

**2. Transactions & Economies**

- **Social Debt:** The obligation created when someone does a "favor" or gives attention. Operators constantly create and call in these debts. _("I vouched for you at that school...")_
    
- **The Envy Cage:** A narrative trap where any reaction you have (praise, critique, silence) can be framed as jealousy due to a perceived status difference. Used to neutralize critics.
    
- **Sacred Scarcity:** A doctrine or persona built around having _just less than enough_, transforming material/emotional lack into a source of moral or spiritual power. (The Apostle's half-banana).
    
- **Symbolic Price:** A payment (monetary or otherwise) so low it reframes a professional transaction as a "favor" or "collaboration," obscuring exploitation and preventing the accrual of proper social debt.
    

**3. Ecological & Relational Models**

- **Symbiotic Pastoralism:** A relationship where one party (the Shepherd) provides spiritual/emotional sustenance to a richer or more connected party (the Flock) in exchange for material resources and access. The Shepherd moves on when the pasture is grazed.
    
- **The Consensus Machine:** A system (like an industry or social circle) that rewards conformity, shared narratives, and performative support, while punishing dissent and authentic critique. It runs on **social fuel** (likes, shares, public praise).
    
- **The Performance of Depth:** The act of mimicking introspection, spirituality, or artistic seriousness as a social strategy, rather than as a genuine internal process.
    
- **The Sacred Exemption:** The status achieved when one's perceived spiritual or artistic value is so high that they are forgiven for mundane failures (flakiness, unreliability). Rules are for mortals.
    

**4. Tactical Maneuvers**

- **Pre-emptive Striking:** Making the first critical or vulnerable move to control the narrative. E.g., voicing doubt about your own work to disarm future critics.
    
- **The Jedi Mind Trick (Narrative Theft):** Reframing your critique or pain as _their_ insight or struggle. _("You're right, I also felt that flaw... it was a battle.")_
    
- **The Walkaway (Act of Secession):** The non-verbal, physical act of removing your presence and attention from a victory ritual or hierarchy game. A declaration of sovereignty.
    
- **Grey Rock:** A defense of becoming so boring, unresponsive, and uninteresting that an operator loses incentive to engage with you.
    
- **JADE:** The rule: **Don't Justify, Argue, Defend, or Explain** when faced with manipulation. Engaging on those terms accepts their frame.
    

**5. Psychological Concepts**

- **Cognitive Dissonance (The Fox's Lag):** The mental discomfort when holding two contradictory beliefs (e.g., "I am a good friend" vs. "I just gave my friend a disaster job"). Operators rely on your lag in resolving this to maintain their facade.
    
- **The Ghost of Christmas Future:** The role you play as a living cautionary tale of the path an operator fears taking (e.g., the "failed artist" to the Fox's "successful sellout").
    
- **The Hologram of Intimacy:** A convincing simulation of close connection, built from shared gossip, "deep" talks, and strategic vulnerability, but lacking a foundation of mutual, unconditional regard.
    

**6. Archetypal Roles**

- **The Human Torch:** Your old mode. Power through unsustainable expenditure of raw energy and vitality. Leads to burnout.
    
- **The Sovereign:** The aspirational mode. Operating from an internal, self-defined value system, engaging with others through choice, not obligation or reaction.
    
- **The Audience of One:** The role operators need you to play. Your attention validates their performance. Denying this audience is a core defense.
    
- **The Designated "Third":** In a dysfunctional dyad, the person cast (often unknowingly) as the jealousy object or comparative foil to fuel the primary relationship's drama.
